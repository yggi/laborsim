# Downstream mechanics

Spilled from `doc/MEMORY.md` § 7. These are the mechanics that *fall out* of the
core commitments rather than being designed alongside them — which is the
argument for each of them. Source: `HANDOVER.md` § 3.

---

## Phantom Labor

The antagonist attacks **the sensor surface that capability created** —
scrambling instruments, not armour.

Difficulty curve and antagonist become the same object: no separate balance
pass, no bolted-on villain. It also makes the machinery ladder **non-monotonic**
— the two-lever tracked cage is the one machine that cannot be scrambled, so the
right answer to a hot site is sometimes to bring the excavator.

That is Patlabor's thesis, arrived at from mechanics rather than script, and it
is why rung one must stay a genuinely good machine and never a tutorial.

## Hot-patching, anchored on LOTO

Lockout–tagout is a real procedure with a real cost, which is exactly why it
prices field repair without arbitrary fragility dice.

- **Lock outputs** — parks the actuator and holds state. Safe, inert, behind
  schedule.
- **Rewire live** — gambles on transient authority handoff. Get the order wrong
  on a stabiliser and the Labor goes limp and falls.

It gets more dangerous the more advanced the Labor, which is the same
capability-costs-you-something shape as the panel budget and the chase camera.
It is also exactly the trade a real field tech makes, so the fiction is
documentary rather than invented.

## Component curriculum

Every rung-one component needs a named **rung-two successor visible on the shelf
from day one and unaffordable.**

Waypoint-drives-into-a-ditch is funny once. It is a *game* when the ditch sends
you back to build for a slope-aware variant **and the load chart moves by a
number you can read.**

Curriculum and economy in the same object — which is what lets v0 ship without
an economy at all and still leave room for one.
