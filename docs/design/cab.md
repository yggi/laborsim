# The cab — the glass, the kit bolted to it, and what it costs you

Start here for anything the operator sees, hears or touches from the seat. The
governing fact is `MEMORY.md` § 6: **every instrument installed obscures your
direct view**, and that is the rare case where the UX constraint *is* the game
constraint.

Principle 7 draws the line this cluster sits on. The world may look like a
simulation — bare contours, an obvious grid — because in the fiction it is one.
The cab may not: it is the real thing you sit in, and it gets the wear, the
weight, the grain and the wrong kind of light.

## Where to go instead

- What the instruments are *reading* is [the machine](machine.md) — a limit
  belongs there, its needle belongs here.
- The rig's own surfaces — the debrief, the schedule, the objective strip — are
  **not cab kit** and cost no glass. They are in [the rig](rig.md), and the
  boundary is `MEMORY.md` § 11: if a manufacturer built it, `cockpit/`; if the
  training system built it, `ui/`.
- The one-directional snapshot boundary that lets an instrument drive a replay
  is [`code/architecture-rules.md`](code/architecture-rules.md), rule 3.

## The shape of it

The cab is a **budget**. Glass is finite, attention is finite, and every page
here is about spending one of them — which is why a component can be refused for
want of view, and why a voice has to earn a quantity before it is allowed to
make a noise.
